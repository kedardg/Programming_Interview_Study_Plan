This chapter is the model side of the story. From raw text to next token prediction: tokenization, embeddings, the transformer block stage by stage, why deep stacks train at all, the training objective, sampling, and how the architecture evolved from G P T two to DeepSeek V three. By the end, when you read a model card, you will be able to translate every line of it into compute, memory, and bytes per user.

A language model never sees letters. It sees a sequence of integers, each naming an entry in a fixed vocabulary of strings called tokens. The dominant algorithm is byte pair encoding, B P E, and it builds the vocabulary by brute statistics. Start with single bytes. Find the most frequent adjacent pair in a training corpus. Merge it into a new token. Repeat tens of thousands of times. Frequent words like "the" and "ing" and "and" end up as single tokens; rare words split into familiar pieces. The learned merge list is the tokenizer. To encode new text, apply the merges greedily in the order they were learned. The page has an interactive where you type text and drag a slider from zero to thirty merges; at zero every character is its own token, and as you drag, frequent pairs fuse before your eyes.

The design trade off is vocabulary size. Llama three uses one hundred twenty eight thousand tokens; G P T two used fifty thousand. A bigger vocabulary means fewer tokens per sentence, which means less compute and less K V cache per request, since every cost in the inference chapter scales with token count. The price is a bigger embedding table and a bigger final projection. English averages roughly three and a half to four and a half characters per token with modern tokenizers.

Tokenization explains several famous quirks. Models struggle to count the r's in strawberry because they never see letters. The word may be one or two tokens, and the model must have memorized facts about its spelling rather than reading it. Arithmetic suffers when digit sequences split inconsistently, which is why modern tokenizers special case digits. And a trailing space can change everything, because "space the" and "the" are different tokens with different statistics.

Next, embeddings: integers become vectors. Each token id indexes a row of an embedding matrix of shape vocabulary size by model width. For Llama three eight B that row is four thousand ninety six numbers. It is learned like every other weight, so tokens that behave similarly end up with nearby vectors, and directions in the space encode meaning. From here on, the entire transformer is a machine that repeatedly refines these vectors. The vector at position i gradually stops meaning "the token at position i" and starts meaning "everything the context implies at position i."

One problem. Attention, the only operation that mixes positions, is permutation invariant. Shuffle the input vectors and you shuffle the outputs identically. The model cannot know word order unless positions are injected explicitly. G P T two added a learned vector per absolute position. Modern models instead rotate queries and keys by position dependent angles, the rotary position embedding, rope, which encodes relative position. The attention chapter animates it.

Now the transformer block itself. A decoder only language model is an embedding layer, N identical blocks, thirty two for an eight B model and sixty one for DeepSeek V three, and a final projection back to vocabulary logits. Each block does two things. Attention, where positions exchange information. And an M L P, where each position processes what it gathered, independently. Both are wrapped in residual connections and preceded by normalization. That is the whole trick, repeated N times.

Read the stages precisely. The block input x goes through R M S Norm, then attention, and the result is added back to x. Call that h. Then h goes through R M S Norm, then the M L P, and the result is added back to h. Attention computes queries, keys and values as three linear projections of the normalized input, forms the score matrix Q times K transposed, divides by the square root of the head dimension, adds the causal mask, applies softmax, and multiplies by V. The M L P in modern models is SwiGLU: the SiLU of a gate projection, multiplied elementwise by an up projection, then sent through a down projection, with a hidden width about two point seven to four times the model width.

The page animates five tokens flowing through one block. Watch the attention stage build the five by five score matrix. The greyed upper triangle is the causal mask: position i may only attend to positions at or before i. That is what makes the model autoregressive, and it is also what makes the K V cache possible, because the past never changes. Softmax turns each surviving row into weights, and the weighted sum of value vectors is what each token learns from its past. Two facts about the block worth holding: the M L P holds about two thirds of the parameters in a dense model and is where factual knowledge appears to be stored, while attention is the routing system that moves information to where the M L P can use it. And the division by the square root of the head dimension keeps dot products in a range where softmax has useful gradients.

Why do one hundred layer stacks train at all? Without residual connections, stacking one hundred layers multiplies one hundred Jacobians and gradients vanish or explode. The residual form, x plus f of x, changes the geometry. There is an identity path from the loss to every layer, so gradients flow undiminished, and each block only has to learn a correction to a running residual stream rather than a full transformation. A productive mental model: the residual stream is a shared communication bus of width d. Attention heads and M L Ps read from it, compute, and write their contribution back by addition.

Normalization keeps the stream's scale stable so corrections from layer ninety are commensurate with those from layer two. Modern models use R M S Norm and place it before each sublayer, called pre norm. Pre norm leaves the residual path completely clean; the identity is never normalized away. That is why G P T two era post norm needed careful warmup while pre norm models train stably at much greater depth.

The objective is self supervised. Every position in every text is a labeled example whose label is simply the next token. The model outputs logits over the vocabulary at every position simultaneously, and the loss is the average cross entropy between each position's prediction and the actual next token. This parallel scoring is called teacher forcing: position i is conditioned on the true prefix, not on the model's own earlier predictions, so the whole sequence trains in one pass. It is the same parallelism that makes prefill fast at inference time, and the causal mask is what makes it legitimate, guaranteeing no position peeks at its own answer.

Perplexity is the exponential of this loss, interpretable as "the model is as uncertain as if choosing uniformly among that many options." A loss of two point zero nats is a perplexity of about seven point four. Frontier models reach low single digits on held out web text. Pretraining at scale is governed by scaling laws, Chinchilla's roughly twenty tokens per parameter compute optimal rule, now routinely exceeded because inference cost rewards over training small models.

At inference the model gives you a distribution and you decide how to pick from it. Greedy decoding takes the argmax: deterministic but repetitive and bland. Temperature divides the logits before softmax; below one it sharpens toward the top tokens, above one it flattens. Top k keeps only the k most likely tokens. Top p, nucleus sampling, keeps the smallest set whose probabilities sum to p, which is adaptive: few tokens when the model is confident, many when it is not. Production samplers compose these: temperature, then truncation, then renormalize and sample. The page's sampling playground shows the distribution after "The capital of France is." Lower the temperature and watch the mass collapse onto Paris. Two practical notes: repetition and frequency penalties exist because pure sampling can loop, and they are heuristics applied to logits, not part of the model. And constrained generation, forcing valid JSON, is sampling with a mask that evolves with a grammar.

Now the architecture tour. The block diagram has been stable since two thousand seventeen; the deltas that matter are small, but together they roughly halve the compute needed to reach a given quality, and several exist specifically to make serving cheaper. G P T two in two thousand nineteen used LayerNorm after each sublayer, GELU activations, learned absolute positions, multi head attention, a dense feed forward, one point five billion parameters at the largest, a one thousand token context and a fifty thousand token vocabulary. Llama three in two thousand twenty four uses R M S Norm pre norm, SwiGLU, rope, grouped query attention with eight K V heads, a dense feed forward, four hundred five billion parameters at the largest, a one hundred twenty eight thousand token context and a one hundred twenty eight thousand token vocabulary. DeepSeek V three, also two thousand twenty four, keeps the norms and SwiGLU, uses rope decoupled for multi head latent attention, M L A with a compressed latent K V, and replaces the feed forward with a mixture of experts: two hundred fifty six routed experts plus one shared expert, eight active per token. Six hundred seventy one billion total parameters but only thirty seven billion active.

Mixture of experts deserves a sentence of intuition. Replace the one big M L P with many smaller expert M L Ps and a learned router that sends each token to its top k experts. Parameters, which is to say knowledge, scale with the number of experts, while per token flops scale only with the k active ones. The shared expert handles common patterns so routed experts can specialize, and auxiliary losses, or V three's bias adjustment trick, keep the load balanced. The price is systems complexity: routing, all to all communication, expert placement. That is exactly the parallelism chapter's problem.

Finally, what a context window costs. "One hundred twenty eight K context" is a systems claim, not just a model claim. Attention flops grow quadratically with context during prefill. The K V cache grows linearly and must survive for the whole generation. At one hundred twenty eight thousand tokens, a Llama three seventy B conversation holds gigabytes of cache per user. Everything in the serving stack, G Q A and M L A, paged allocation, cache quantization, prefix sharing, exists to pay this bill. When you read a model card, translate the context window into bytes per user before you believe the concurrency numbers.

Interview questions and misconceptions. Why does the model need positional information at all? Because attention is permutation invariant. What does teacher forcing mean and why does it make training fast? All positions are scored in one pass conditioned on the true prefix. Why pre norm over post norm? A clean identity path through the residual stream, so deep stacks train stably. What is the difference between top k and top p? Fixed count versus adaptive probability mass. And a subtle one: does a mixture of experts model with six hundred seventy one billion parameters cost six hundred seventy one billion parameters of compute per token? No. Thirty seven billion active. But it does cost that much memory to hold.

One more section, because rapid-fire interviews love it: accounting. Alisa Liu's Book of L L Ms keeps a running page of the formulas for parameters, activations, flops and memory, and this chapter now has that page as a live calculator. Parameters first. Per decoder block, attention is two D squared for the query and output projections plus two times D times K times H for the key and value projections, which is four D squared with full multi-head attention and much less under grouped query attention. A swiglu feed-forward has three matrices, gate, up and down, so three D F, and with the Llama convention of F equal to eight thirds D that is eight D squared. Two R M S norms add two D. Embeddings are V times D, twice if the unembedding is untied as in Llama, once if tied as in G P T two. Run the numbers for Llama three eight B, with a hundred and twenty eight thousand vocabulary, width four thousand ninety six, thirty two layers, eight K V heads and F of fourteen thousand three hundred thirty six, and you get two hundred eighteen million per block, almost seven billion across the stack, plus a billion of embeddings, for eight point zero three billion, exactly the published count. Notice that the feed-forward is eighty percent of every block and embeddings are thirteen percent of the model.

Activations are what training must remember for the backward pass, and the Book's total per layer is fourteen B S D plus B N S squared, the squared term being the attention score matrix, which flash attention removes by recomputing it. That is usually the dominant training memory term, it scales with total tokens, and gradient checkpointing trades it for recompute by keeping only each layer's input. Flops are counted two per multiply-add: the forward pass per token is about two times the non-embedding parameters, plus two D V for the unembedding, plus four L S D for attention over the context, and the backward pass is twice the forward, which is where the six N D rule comes from. Memory closes the loop: inference is weights plus K V cache plus one layer of peak activations, and training in mixed precision costs eighteen bytes per parameter, four for the F P thirty two master weights, two for the B F sixteen working copy, eight for the Adam moments and four for gradient accumulation, before a single activation, which is why a seven billion parameter model needs over a hundred and twenty gigabytes to fine-tune fully.

The same section collects the implementation details people fumble on a whiteboard: masking with negative infinity before the softmax, caching the rope cosines and sines at initialization and rotating queries and keys but never values, the reshape, unbind, transpose dance around multi-head attention, weight tying, depth-scaled initialization, and exactly which pieces stay in F P thirty two. And it closes with gradient flow through sampling: a sampled token is not differentiable, which is why reinforcement learning uses the score function estimator, why the Gumbel softmax trick exists as a stochastic, differentiable relaxation, and why the straight-through estimator pretends a rounding step was the identity, wrong in magnitude but right in direction, exactly as quantization-aware training uses it.
