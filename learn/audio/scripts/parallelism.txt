One G P U is never enough. Models outgrow its memory, and latency targets outgrow its bandwidth. So we split, and every way of splitting is a different answer to one question: what do we divide, and what do we pay in communication? This chapter covers the five collective operations everything is built on, then the four axes of parallelism, tensor, pipeline, data with F S D P, and expert, plus sequence parallelism, and how they compose differently for training and for serving.

First, why one G P U fails. There are two walls. The memory wall: Llama three seventy B in B F sixteen is about one hundred forty gigabytes of weights before a single token of K V cache, and an H one hundred has eighty gigabytes. Training is worse: Adam's sixteen bytes per parameter is one point one terabytes for seventy B before activations. The latency wall: decode speed at batch one is bounded by one sweep of the weights through memory. If seventy gigabytes over three point three five terabytes per second, about twenty one milliseconds per token, is too slow, the only fix is to give the token more aggregate bandwidth, which means sharding the weights across G P Us that read their slices in parallel.

Now the vocabulary. Distributed machine learning is written in five collectives, implemented by nickel on Nvidia hardware. All reduce: every G P U ends with the elementwise sum of all inputs. Reduce scatter keeps only each G P U's one over N shard of that sum. All gather concatenates all shards everywhere. All to all: G P U i sends its j th chunk to G P U j, a transpose across devices. The famous identity is that all reduce equals reduce scatter followed by all gather, and the ring algorithm achieves it with each G P U sending only to its neighbor. The cost is worth memorizing: a ring all reduce moves two times D times N minus one over N bytes per G P U, where D is the data size and N the number of G P Us. For four G P Us that is one and a half times the data. It is bandwidth optimal regardless of N, and it only uses neighbor to neighbor links, which is why rings love N V Link. The ring animation on the page shows the two phases, reduce scatter then all gather.

Bandwidth is hierarchical. Inside a node, N V Link gives roughly nine hundred gigabytes per second between G P Us, an island of eight. Between nodes, InfiniBand or Ethernet gives fifty to one hundred gigabytes per second, an order of magnitude cliff. The art of placement is keeping chatty collectives inside the island and letting only quiet ones cross it.

Tensor parallelism, from the Megatron L M paper by Shoeybi and colleagues at Nvidia in twenty nineteen, splits individual weight matrices so that every G P U computes a slice of every layer for every token. For a transformer M L P, output equals the activation function of X times A, then times B. Split A by columns and B by rows. Each G P U computes its column slice of X times A, applies the nonlinearity independently, since it acts per element, then multiplies by its row slice of B to get a partial sum of the output. One all reduce makes the output exact on every G P U. The column then row pairing is the trick: it defers all communication to a single all reduce per M L P, and one per attention block, where heads are simply dealt out across G P Us, each attending over its own K V shard.

The cost of tensor parallelism is that it communicates activations, batch by sequence by hidden size, twice per layer for every microbatch. It is the chattiest parallelism there is. That is why the tensor parallel degree stays inside the N V Link island, with T P equals eight as the classic choice, and why it is the tool for the latency wall: T P four gives a decode token four times the aggregate memory bandwidth, directly cutting time per output token. Beyond the island, the all reduce over slow links erases the gain.

Pipeline parallelism cuts the model into stages of consecutive layers: G P U zero holds layers one to twenty, G P U one holds twenty one to forty, and so on. Communication is tiny, just one activation tensor handed to the next stage, so pipelines cross nodes happily. The price is the bubble: stages idle while the pipeline fills and drains. Splitting the batch into m microbatches and scheduling one forward then one backward keeps memory flat and shrinks the idle fraction to p minus one over m plus p minus one, with p stages and m microbatches. With four stages and eight microbatches that is about twenty seven percent idle.

Data parallelism replicates the whole model on every G P U, feeds each a different slice of the batch, and all reduces gradients once per step. Compute scales beautifully; memory does not, because every replica carries the full sixteen bytes per parameter of training state. The ZeRO paper by Rajbhandari and colleagues at Microsoft in twenty nineteen observed that this state is redundant, so shard it and fetch on demand. ZeRO one shards optimizer states, ZeRO two also gradients, and ZeRO three, which PyTorch's F S D P implements, also the parameters themselves: each layer's weights are all gathered just in time, used, and freed, so only one full layer lives in memory at a time, and prefetching overlaps the next gather with the current compute. H S D P is the two level version, sharding inside each node and replicating across nodes, so the heavy gathers stay on N V Link.

Expert parallelism is for mixture of experts models, where a router activates the top k of many smaller M L Ps per token; DeepSeek V three has two hundred fifty six routed experts with eight active. Since only a fraction fire per token, the natural distribution is one or a few experts per G P U, and tokens must physically travel to their experts. Dispatch is an all to all that sends each token to the G P U hosting its routed experts. Combine is a second all to all that brings results back to the token's home G P U for the weighted sum. The hazard is load: if the router loves expert two, G P U two drowns while the others idle. Load balancing is the central M o E systems problem, attacked with auxiliary losses at training time and with E P L B style expert replication and placement from measured load at serving time. DeepEP is DeepSeek's kernel library for these all to alls.

Long contexts blow up activations and K V along the sequence axis, so shard that: each G P U holds a contiguous chunk of the sequence. The problem is attention, where every query must see every key. Ulysses re shards with all to alls: before attention, flip from sequence sharded to head sharded, so each G P U gets the full sequence for a subset of heads and attention is exact and local, then flip back. Ring attention keeps sequence shards and circulates K and V blocks around a ring, each G P U accumulating partial attention with online softmax merging as blocks pass by. Axolotl exposes both for long context fine tuning.

Now compose it. Training builds an N dimensional grid: for example four thousand ninety six G P Us as tensor parallel eight inside nodes, times pipeline parallel sixteen, times data parallel thirty two, with expert parallelism folded into the M o E layers and ZeRO sharding the data parallel axis. The placement rule, in order of communication appetite: tensor parallelism inside the N V Link island, expert parallelism and F S D P next, pipeline and plain data parallelism across nodes. Inference composes differently because there is no backward pass and latency bounds everything. Use tensor parallelism for latency, to multiply decode bandwidth. Use replicas for throughput: once a tensor parallel group meets the latency objective, scale out with independent copies behind a router, and never widen T P further than the objective requires. And disaggregate prefill and decode into pools with different T P degrees, as the inference chapter describes.

Here is the worked example you should be able to do in an interview without notes. Serve Llama seventy B in F P eight on four H one hundreds with tensor parallel four. Weights: seventy gigabytes divided by four is seventeen and a half gigabytes per G P U. Subtract about five gigabytes of workspace, leaving about fifty seven gigabytes for cache per G P U. The K V per token in F P eight is two times eighty layers times eight K V heads times one hundred twenty eight dimensions times one byte, one hundred sixty kilobytes, which is forty kilobytes per G P U at T P four. Fifty seven gigabytes divided by forty kilobytes is about one point four million tokens of cache, roughly one hundred seventy five concurrent eight K context sequences. Every term came from this chapter or from the K V cache formula.

Across nodes, topology is destiny: a tensor parallel group spanning two nodes silently halves throughput, and U A Link and Ultra Ethernet are the standardizing scale up and scale out fabrics.

Common misconceptions. More parallelism is not more speed: each axis has a communication tax, and past the point where the tax exceeds the gain you slow down. Tensor parallelism and pipeline parallelism are not interchangeable: one trades bandwidth for latency, the other trades bubbles for memory. The interview question that tests all of it is: how would you serve a seventy B model on eight G P Us with a two hundred millisecond first token target and fifty millisecond per token target? Answer with the arithmetic above, then explain which axis you would change if either target failed.
