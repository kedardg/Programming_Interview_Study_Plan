Aditya Verma's Patterns → Blind 75 and NeetCode 150

The study plan sends you to Aditya Verma's channel whenever a problem beats the 45-minute cap. His playlists are in Hindi/Hinglish and teach patterns, not problems: each starts with "how do I identify this?", then a parent problem, then every variation as a small edit of the parent. This page reconstructs that taxonomy from his playlists, maps every Blind 75 and NeetCode 150 problem onto it, and gives each video a slot for its transcript.

1. How he teaches, and how to use the videos

Every playlist follows the same arc. First, identification: what in the problem statement tells you this is a sliding-window problem, a knapsack problem, a monotonic-stack problem? He states the cue explicitly ("subarray or substring + a condition or a window size" → sliding window; "choice at every element + optimize or count" → knapsack DP; "nearest greater/smaller element to the left or right" → stack). Second, a parent problem solved from first principles: the brute-force recursion written out with its choice diagram, then memoization added as a two-line change, then the bottom-up table derived by reading the memoized code. Third, variations: a dozen problems that are each the parent with one line changed, so that by the end you recognize the family rather than memorizing solutions.

Use the videos the way the plan intends: hit the 45-minute cap, watch the identification and parent videos for the pattern (not the exact problem), close the video, and re-solve with AI assistance off — the muscle-memory rule from Alisa Liu's notes in the Interview Journey chapter applies to LeetCode exactly as it does to ML coding. The templates below are his approaches written in idiomatic Python; the Coding Patterns chapter has full solutions for all 75 problems and the Coding Patterns flashcard deck drills the moves.
